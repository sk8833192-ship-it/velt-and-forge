How is glass clearance calculated?

For square ice, the diagonal is the side length multiplied by the square root of two. We reserve at least 8 mm total between that diagonal and the measured glass inner diameter: 4 mm at each opposing corner. Rectangular blocks use the full corner-to-corner diagonal. Measure the narrowest part of a tapered glass at the ice position.

Why does a 50 mm stamp not fit a 50 mm cube?

The full head needs a clean 4 mm ice margin on each side. Our nominal 50 mm option uses the catalog’s 50.8 mm physical envelope, so it needs a flat ice face at least 58.8 mm wide. The nominal 38 mm and 63 mm heads need at least 46.1 mm and 71.5 mm respectively. Confirm production dimensions in your workshop proof.

Can I stamp a clear ice sphere or a highball spear?

An uncut sphere has a curved surface and no safe flat stamping face. Cut and measure a stable flat face before choosing a head. A highball spear often has a top too narrow even for a 38 mm stamp; its height and glass taper also need a separate physical check.

How long should brass and stainless steel touch the ice?

Start with 2–3 seconds for lead-free bismuth brass or 4–5 seconds for 304 stainless steel. Temper ice for about 3–5 minutes until frost clears, use a dry room-temperature head, press lightly and lift vertically. These are starting estimates; rehearse with your actual ice, artwork and service conditions.

What does the recommended stamp cost?

Brass base prices are $69 for 1.5 inches (38 mm), $89 for 2.0 inches (50 mm) and $109 for 2.5 inches (63 mm). A 304 stainless steel head adds $15. An optional 304 steel handle adds $18 and an extra M8 interchangeable custom head adds $49. Prices are in USD; the calculator total includes only the selected head material.

Is this calculator free and private?

Yes. The calculator runs in your browser without signup, uploads or external dependencies, including when opened as a local file. It estimates geometry and technique; verify actual glass, ice and workshop dimensions before ordering.

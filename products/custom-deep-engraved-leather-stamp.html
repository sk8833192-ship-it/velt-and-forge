Why depth matters on leather

No halo.
Just your mark.

When a stamp is pressed into cased vegetable-tanned leather, the raised mark sinks in, often a millimetre or more on 4–10 oz hides. If the relief is shallow, the stamp’s recessed background reaches the leather too and compresses a dull outline around your mark: the background halo, or bruising, that makes a maker’s mark look cheap.

We cut to a relief depth target of at least 2.8 mm, so the background floor stays clear of the leather even on thick, well-cased hides. Only the mark itself is debossed, with crisp walls and a clean field around it.

Depth isn’t the whole story. Fine lines still need room. We screen strokes and gaps at your final size and show you the proof before machining.

  1. 01

    Case it.

    Dampen the grain side evenly with a sponge. Wait until the color comes back almost to natural and the surface feels cool, not wet. Too wet and the edges go soft; too dry and the mark is shallow.

  2. 02

    Set it square.

    Leather on a stone slab, stamp flat and square. Mallet: hold the rod upright and strike once, squarely. Press: bring the ram down with steady, even pressure.

  3. 03

    Strike once. Let it dry.

    Avoid a second strike; it rarely lands in the same place and doubles the edges. Let the leather dry fully before dye or finish.

Is one edge deep while the rest stays faint? Fix uneven impressions on a large brass leather stamp with ram alignment checks, a hardened steel load-spreader plate, even casing and a controlled two-stage dwell.

Before you
make your mark.

As the raised mark sinks into cased 4–10 oz leather, a shallow stamp’s background floor reaches the surface too and presses a dull outline, a halo, around the mark. A relief depth of at least 2.8 mm keeps the background clear of the leather, so only your mark is debossed.

A 1.5-inch face works well with one firm strike from a poly or rawhide maul. For 2-inch faces an arbor press gives a more even impression, and for 2.5-inch faces we recommend an arbor or hydraulic press. The included mallet rod and the M8-tapped head support both.
